## Chapters 2 to 5: what each needs, at the least

For each: the questions that have to be answered before a design like Chapter 1's can be written. Where a proposal is offered it is the smallest one that fits what the canon already says; any of them can be struck.

### 02 Three Roads to Ruin

What is fixed: three towns, three troubles, any order, and one road each by road, rail and river. Chapter 1 already hands the three over in Pritchard's last line, and "Signed Clean" already points at Iron Junction's yard.

Needed for each of the three:

1. **Who the player talks to there.** One person who wants the trouble solved and one who does not. (Chapter 1 managed with six people in a town.)
2. **What is actually wrong.** Greenwater's food is missing: stolen, sold, spoiled, or never delivered? Red Hollow's mine is sealed: by whom, against what? Iron Junction's rails are cut: by whom, for whose gain?
3. **Whether the three are one story.** The strongest reading of what exists: all three are the same hand, somebody making sure the valleys cannot feed, dig or move without help. That would lead straight into Chapter 4 (Vane's financing "to preserve local isolation"). The other reading is three unrelated troubles and a tired sheriff. The first costs more thought now and saves Chapter 4.
4. **What the player decides in each**, and what it changes later. The chapter's rule from the doctrine: a choice shown by its consequences.
5. **How "Signed Clean" lands.** If the ledger was reported, the yard has been warned (the flag exists). If it was sold, Vane has it. Iron Junction should open differently in each case.

Proposal for a first step that commits to little: write Iron Junction first, because Chapter 1 already has its hooks (Line 14, the dead inspector R. Ostrander, the yard office, the alerted-yard flag), and let it settle question 3.

### 03 The Four Flags

Needed: which four (the name suggests the four principal factions' local faces, but in Act I nobody knows of the Deep, so who carries the fourth flag?); where they meet; who the messenger is and what "mountain intelligence" means to people who do not know what is in the mountain; who fires the silver cartridge, and whether the player can know by the end of the chapter or only by the end of the act.

A caution rather than a proposal: this is the first chapter where the larger factions show themselves, so it is where a fifth faction could slip in by accident. Whatever is written should name which of the four principal and four ancillary factions each "flag" belongs to.

### 04 The Black Ledger

What exists: Silas Vane stands in Mercy Crossing from Chapter 1, and one outcome of "Signed Clean" puts a falsified ledger in his hands. The canon says he finances isolation and can be exposed, pressured or recruited.

Needed: who he finances and with whose money; how the player gets to see his books (and a way that does not need the side quest); what each of the three endings costs. Chapter 1's rule that he is never shown guilty or innocent has to end here, by design.

### 05 The Fifth Rider

Not to be drafted yet (see above). What can be prepared without touching the protagonist: Blackstone Station as a place; what "the Corridor line keeps losing marshals" (Pritchard, Chapter 1) has to do with it; and the decision in question 3 of the Chapter 1 table, which decides how much the player may suspect before this chapter.

## Mercy Crossing as a place

The canon has its people and its trades. It has no look and no plan: how big, built of what, on which bank of what, where the gate and the yard are. The Chapter 1 design names three interiors (the warden's office, the trading post, the clinic) and "a short street with a gate and a notice board". That is enough for a first built version, and it is the next thing a walk-through needs once the rough cut has been played: the test room has no street.

A layout for it is one of the things asked of the image generator in [ACT1_chatgpt_asset_brief.md](ACT1_chatgpt_asset_brief.md). A drawn plan is a proposal like any other until the owner accepts it.

## What would help most, in order

1. **Play the rough cut** and say what is wrong with it. An hour of that will settle more than a page of questions.
2. **Decide question 3 of Chapter 2** (one hand behind the three troubles, or not). Chapters 2 and 4 hang on it.
3. **Approve or change the six names**, so that nothing more is built on names that may move.
4. **Settle the choices in child-of-the-deep.md**, which unlocks Chapter 5 and the protagonist's own lines everywhere.
5. Pick one of the three towns to design in full next. Proposed: Iron Junction.
