# Act I character and dialogue development

Status: PROPOSED, 2026-11-14. Companion to [the storyboard](ACT1_storyboard_and_scene_development.md). All new personality details, ages, histories, business practices, lines and stage directions here are proposals. Existing names retain their approval status in the naming registry and MQ01 design. No dialogue has been inserted into the game.

## Level three writing and performance

Sophistication comes from a person pursuing something in the scene, knowing only part of the situation and choosing what to leave unsaid. Vocabulary may be ordinary. Reserve memorable lines for earned turns; a town full of people speaking in polished maxims will sound like one author.

Each exchange needs an immediate task. Let people interrupt themselves to work, misunderstand a limited point, answer only what they were asked, or change a claim when shown evidence. A frightened person may become precise. An honest person may be evasive about something private. A convincing liar often supplies accurate peripheral detail.

Use southern Appalachian cadence lightly through sentence rhythm and concrete terms. Do not spell an accent phonetically. Avoid treating poverty, age, disability or education as shorthand for goodness, intelligence or deceit. The proposed ages below organize differences in experience; none is a locked portrait specification.

The player has action or topic selections, never authored spoken lines. Every important exchange has a way to defer or leave. A character can draw a tentative social impression from silence, but cannot treat it as the player's completed answer.

Pritchard and Vane can respect each other's competence without sharing aims. Pike and Keene disagree about keeping Talbot out of danger: one wants him trained, the other wants an extra pair of reliable hands now. Nell and Haskell understand that a receipt can be both correct and misleading about a journey. These relationships should exist when the wanderer is absent.

## New Chapter 2 roles

The yard supervisor is older, capable and compromised by a decision that became a habit. The apprentice fitter is younger and factually correct about a defect but initially careless about what the defect proves about intent. Neither should be written as the other's moral prop.

The Greenwater custodian is administratively strong and privately frightened. The river porter has little standing but specific competence in landing and storing cargo. The custodian must learn to hear an inconvenient account; the porter must account for sacks moved without permission.

The Red Hollow pump keeper has expertise and physical limitations. The mine delegate can negotiate forcefully yet cannot make families accept indefinite unemployment. Do not make the pump keeper a mystical elder or the delegate a greedy fool.

A role can perform an action and have a personality before a new proper name is chosen. These are new roles, not additions to the canonical named cast.

## Replacement candidates for Chapter 1

The source dialogue is in TLM_ACT1_first_section.md, What people say. These are wording candidates for a later synchronized implementation pass. Preserve existing topic meanings, timing, rewards and outcome conditions.

### Dunmore at the wagon

Opening, unresolved hold-up:

DUNMORE: "Keep moving. This is between us and the wagon."

[Ask about a share]

DUNMORE: "Half the load. The drover dies."

[Accept] retains the existing exploit-deal meaning and its fatal consequence for Abel. The offer states the fatal consequence before acceptance; staging must also make the threat legible.

[Refuse]

DUNMORE: "Then you\'re in the way."

This refusal line accompanies the existing turn to hostility at stage 37.

[Invoke the sheriff's threat]

Success: "All right. We're leaving."
Failure: "Try bringing him out here."

The player selection retains the existing warning meaning. No new intimidation mechanic is proposed by these lines.

NELL, before resolution: "Abel. Leave the brake. Come over here."

ABEL, before resolution: "If I let it off, the wagon goes."

The situation is physical and the pair have a working relationship. Their exchange is optional background, never a new timed condition for saving them.

### Nell after rescue

NELL: "Abel's checking the harness. We'll know in a minute if we can move."

[Stay]

NELL: "Here. It's what I can spare."

[Accept payment] uses the existing single reward.
[Decline payment] preserves refusal.
[Ask who attacked] uses the existing identification.

NELL: "Dunmore. Tellico Cutters. They've been waiting where the road narrows."

The last sentence is a proposed observation about this encounter, not proof of a history of robberies.

ABEL, if alive after rescue: "Brake's holding. I can get us as far as the yard."

Later, optional and only after immediate danger has passed:

ABEL: "She'll pay to mend the harness. The wagon can complain for free."

Use one small work joke, not a running quip in every exchange.

### Nell after loss or betrayal

After witnessed evasion, retain the existing core line:

NELL: "You watched. You kept walking."

Then the existing options or silence. No follow-up speech interprets the player's answer as forgiveness.

After missed encounter:

NELL: "Abel's dead. They took what we were carrying."

This reports the result without accusing the player of choosing to watch. Any question about how she reached town belongs in optional conversation.

After exploitation, Nell continues refusing conversation. Proposed stage direction: she resumes sorting usable straps; no exaggerated shudder or melodramatic walk away.

KEENE, when the player seeks Nell: "She's awake. She doesn't want to see you."

This is a proposed variant only where the medical and witness conditions support it.

### Pritchard receives the dispatch

PRITCHARD: "Gantt's case. Set it here."

[Deliver] retains the existing hand-over.
[Defer] leaves the existing route available.

PRITCHARD, after delivery: "Did anyone else come out of the wreck?"

This is an optional topic invitation, not a new truth test. [No account to give] and [Leave it] remain available. Do not infer a casualty count from silence.

Saved caravan:

PRITCHARD: "Fenwick's wagon got through. Both of them?"

Use the factual answer only when the actual survivors are known. If Abel or Nell died, use a variant that states the loss instead. Do not let a rescue flag override later deaths.

PRITCHARD: "Obliged. I still need someone to look at the roads."

Evasion, Nell's account available:

PRITCHARD: "Nell says you were there. Abel didn't make it."

Existing admission, denial and silence remain. Avoid a threat of imprisonment the quest does not support.

Exploitation, Nell alive:

PRITCHARD: "Nell's given her account. She says you took a share."

Existing confession, blame, denial and silence remain. Use Abel's name and pronouns when he is the surviving witness. With no surviving witness, use only the existing absence-of-testimony framing.

PRITCHARD, returning to practical work: "Greenwater's short of food. Red Hollow's mine is shut. Rails are being cut at Iron Junction. I can send word. I can't send people."

[Choose a destination] is a proposed presentation of the existing chapter hook; it does not start a new implemented quest.

### Pike and the limits of expertise

PIKE: "Set it on the rag. Let me see the wear."

[Ask about the brake shoes]

PIKE: "The yard bought from me every season. Nothing since spring. This is my book."

[Compare with the railway ledger]

PIKE: "Mine tells you what I sold. If they bought elsewhere, you'll need that book too."

This adds evidentiary restraint without weakening the existing act of acquiring E4. It does not change the computed evidence rules.

[Ask who forged the entry]

PIKE: "I don't know. I didn't see it written."

Optional ambient work exchange:

TALBOT: "That pin doesn't sit straight."
PIKE: "Good. Now tell me which side is worn."

A young person can be teachable without being a comic incompetent.

### Vane before the later reveal

VANE: "What have you brought in?"

[Offer the records when sale is allowed]

VANE: "I'll buy the pages together. You can keep them if you'd rather."

[Ask why]

VANE: "A disputed account still has a price."

This is deliberately equivocal: a merchant purchasing useful information, not a confession. Retain the existing sale conditions and payment.

After purchase:

VANE: "Count it before you go."

The line also suits an honest transaction. Do not direct the actor to betray the plot through a knowing smile.

### Haskell before and after an accusation

HASKELL: "Put the freight slips here. Inspection papers go to the yard."

[Ask about his signature]

HASKELL: "I counted the crates when they came off. I didn't inspect the train."

Optional background, before any accusation:

HASKELL: "This says four. There's three on the floor. Leave it open till the fourth turns up."

He is doing his job, rather than introducing himself as the innocent man.

Later repair scene, proposed and outside the slice:

HASKELL: "You put my name up where everyone could read it."

[Offer the correction]

HASKELL: "Put that there too. Leave it long enough."

[Offer restitution]

HASKELL: "I lost work. We can talk about that. First fix the notice."

He can accept correction or payment without being required to resume friendship.

### Keene and Talbot outside the player's conversation

KEENE: "The clean cloth, Jonah. On the upper shelf."
TALBOT: "All of it?"
KEENE: "Two pieces. Put the rest back dry."

Later callback:

TALBOT: "Two clean pieces. Upper shelf's nearly empty."
KEENE: "Thank you. Mark it down before you forget."

Their changed efficiency communicates a relationship. They do not praise the wanderer for witnessing it.

## Chapter 2 dialogue scenes

### The yard supervisor admits a limited wrong

APPRENTICE: "That was condemned before the train went out."
SUPERVISOR: "I know when it was condemned."
APPRENTICE: "Then why's it on the release sheet?"
SUPERVISOR: "Because I signed the exception."

[Examine the authorization]

SUPERVISOR: "There was medicine on the next run. I thought we'd get the part before another."

[Ask about later runs]

SUPERVISOR: "We didn't. I let them go."

[Ask about Ostrander's signature]

SUPERVISOR: "I can answer for mine. That one needs its own answer."

The supervisor's last sentence may sound evasive; evidence must establish the distinction. Do not let the line itself clear the supervisor of forgery.

Later, after accepting oversight:

SUPERVISOR: "The defect notice is signed."
APPRENTICE: "And the next release?"
SUPERVISOR: "Not mine alone."

Victim or worker response, optional:

WORKER: "I'll work beside you. I won't take your word for the brakes."

This allows cooperation while preserving mistrust.

### Greenwater and a small act with mixed consequences

CUSTODIAN: "The receipt says thirty sacks."
PORTER: "Thirty landed. Six were getting wet."
CUSTODIAN: "Where are they?"
PORTER: "In the loft. I wasn't leaving them there."

[Inspect the sacks]

CUSTODIAN: "They're dry."
PORTER: "I told you."
CUSTODIAN: "You didn't tell me where."

[Help record and deliver the moved grain]

CUSTODIAN: "Six from the loft. Put that down separately."

No uplifting speech is needed. The custodian changes the record to fit the food. The porter remains entitled to fair pay rather than gratitude alone.

### Red Hollow and meaningful weakness

DELEGATE: "There are people ready to go back in."
PUMP KEEPER: "Then come look at the intake."

[Follow to the safe inspection point]

PUMP KEEPER: "I can show you what failed. I can't lift that cover now."

[Assist] or [Find another pair of hands] are equally legitimate. Do not turn disability into a moral test of whether the player personally performs lifting.

DELEGATE, after inspection: "How long?"
PUMP KEEPER: "For the repair? I need the part before I can tell you."

The expert admits uncertainty. The delegate's concern for livelihoods remains reasonable.

## Chapter 3 dialogue at the freight table

COMPACT NEGOTIATOR: "Each carrier keeps their own load. We share the guard bill."

PROVISIONING DELEGATE: "I can send grain. I need people to unload it and a count at the store."

DOMINION OFFICER: "Two escorts on the road. My people search wagons at the gate."

FIELD TECHNICIAN: "I can examine the pump. Whoever maintains it needs to be there."

[Inspect the terms] offers specific questions about payment, duration, inspection or withdrawal. Avoid four competing speeches.

After the assassination:

DOMINION OFFICER: "Clear the shed. One entrance."
PROVISIONING DELEGATE: "The injured stay. Move those crates instead."
COMPACT NEGOTIATOR: "There's room behind my wagon."
FIELD TECHNICIAN: "Bring the lamp here."

They disagree about methods while helping. The scene becomes human before it becomes faction exposition.

## Chapter 4 Vane confrontation

[Present the fee receipt and transfer record]

VANE: "The money is mine. The hold was paid for."

[Ask what the payment purchased]

VANE: "Time. A carrier tied up downriver couldn't take the outside contract."

[Show the local losses]

VANE: "I expected a delay. Not that long."

[Continue to the documented authorizations]

VANE: "Yes. I renewed it."

The admission escalates as the evidence does. The player does not need a scripted rebuttal or a speech about the morality of commerce.

[Ask his reason]

VANE: "They come through with twice the wagons we have. By winter they set the rates. By spring you can't afford to refuse."

[Examine his own terms]

VANE: "You think I haven't done the same."
Pause while he reads the entry.
VANE: "I can see why you would."

The last exchange is proposed self-recognition, not a guarantee that he is reformed. A later action must test it.

[Offer oversight and restitution]

VANE: "Who sees the books?"
[Specify an independent reviewer]
VANE: "And if we disagree?"
[Require the dispute to remain recorded]
VANE: "Leave the terms. I'll read them."

Do not end with immediate friendship. If accepted later, show the first costly compliance: he releases a profitable hold before receiving assurance that the player will defend him.

## Dialogue review checklist

- Can a reader identify the speaker without the name on most lines?
- Does a line answer a practical need or change a relationship?
- Does each accusation stay within this character's evidence?
- Does silence remain available without secretly choosing a moral stance?
- Are witness, death, missed-encounter and prior-sale conditions respected?
- Does a return visit reveal a consequence through work or behavior?
- Can honest people disagree without one becoming secretly corrupt?
- Is atonement tested through a costly act, with forgiveness left to those harmed?
- Are enough exchanges ordinary that the rare memorable line has room to land?

These candidates require a spoken read-through, then a separate implementation review. None has been voice-tested, timed in the game or substituted into the graybox.
